**Root cause: a memory leak in `billing-api`. It isn't a probe or config problem.** The container gets OOMKilled (exit 137) at its 256Mi limit about every 70–80s, which puts it in CrashLoopBackOff.

**Evidence** (live heap profile from the pod's pprof port, 6060):
- About 100% of heap in use comes from one allocation site, `main.renderInvoice` at `/src/main.go:29`, called from the loop in `main.main` at `/src/main.go:50`.
- Each invoice allocates 128 KiB (24,117,248 B / 184 objects), and those buffers are never freed. `HeapAlloc` (255.8 MB) ≈ `TotalAlloc` (256.0 MB), so the GC reclaims almost nothing.
- The numbers match the logs: about 2,000 invoices × 128 KiB ≈ 250 MiB, which is the limit. Every crash happens right after "rendered 2000 invoices". Growth is a steady ~5 MiB/s.

**What to change in the code:**
`renderInvoice` (main.go:29) allocates a 128 KiB buffer per invoice, and something keeps a reference to it. That's most likely an append to a package-level slice, map or cache, or the caller at line 50 collecting the results. To fix it:
1. Stop holding the rendered buffers. Write or send each invoice and drop the reference, rather than collecting them in a global or long-lived slice or map.
2. If you need a cache, put a bound on it (LRU or TTL), or reuse buffers with `sync.Pool` / `buf.Reset()`.
3. Don't keep sub-slices of the big buffer either (`buf[:n]` keeps the whole 128 KiB alive). Copy out just what you need.

I couldn't see the source, so I don't know exactly which line keeps the reference. Look at main.go:29 and wherever line 50 stores its result.

**Mitigation until the fix ships:** raising the memory limit only delays the crash, because growth is unbounded. Rolling back to a version without the leak, if one exists, is the real stopgap.
